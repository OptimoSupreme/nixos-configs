# Hostnames

Every machine I own is named out of Tolkien, and almost all of them from the
wrong side of the war. The server is the Dark Lord, the desktops are his
lieutenants, and the appliances are the odds and ends of his world.

Hostnames must be lowercase ASCII, so the diacritics go: Ossë is `osse`,
Nazgûl is `nazgul`, palantír is `palantir`. Deployment status lives in
`flake.nix`; this file is only about the names.

## In the fleet

### morgoth — server

Melkor was the mightiest of the Ainur and the first Dark Lord; Morgoth,
"Black Foe of the World", is the name the Elves gave him after he stole the
Silmarils. Every evil in the legendarium traces back to him, including
Sauron, the Balrogs and the orcs. The server runs every service the rest of
the fleet depends on, so it gets the top of the org chart. Note that Sauron,
his lieutenant and the Dark Lord of the later ages, is still unclaimed.

### balrog — main desktop

Balrogs were Maiar, spirits of the same order as Gandalf and Sauron, whom
Melkor corrupted before the world was finished. They were the heavy hitters
of his army, the few beings on his side that could kill an elf-lord in single
combat, and they answered to him directly. That is the brief for the primary
desktop: the most powerful machine in the house, and the one most tightly
bound to the server.

### nazgul — laptop

The Nine were kings of Men who took Sauron's rings and faded into wraiths.
They weigh nothing, cast no shadow, and go wherever Sauron sends them, which
is a fair description of a laptop.

### saruman — gaming desktop

Saruman was sent to Middle-earth to oppose Sauron, set himself up in a tower
with a palantír, and ended up feeding Sauron whatever he learned while
pretending to still be on the right side. That is the dual-boot machine: a
NixOS box with a Windows partition that phones home about everything you do.
As with the Nazgûl, "the guy" he reports to is Sauron, not Morgoth. The
nickname Gandalf gives him, Saruman of Many Colours, is a bonus: a wizard who
could not stay one colour, on a machine that cannot stay one operating
system.

### gollum — Steam Machine

Gollum is a hobbit-kin who kept himself alive for five centuries on a ring
and a grudge, hid under a mountain, and challenged visitors to a riddle game
with his dinner as the stake. Old, wasted, and still playing games: a retired
box repurposed as a Steam console.

### palantir — TV

The palantíri were seeing-stones that showed things far away. They were not
evil in themselves, and looking into one was not what hurt you. The danger
was that whoever held the matching stone, Sauron, controlled what you saw and
could bend you through it: Saruman was captured that way, Denethor was worn
down to despair, and Pippin was interrogated after one look. A television is
the same device. It shows you faraway things, and the people on the other end
decide what, which is a better reason to name a TV after it than screen time
alone.

### osse — chartplotter

Ossë is a Maia of the coasts and the shallows, who loves storms and the sound
of breaking waves. He taught the Teleri the craft of shipbuilding and
sailing, which makes him the right patron for a boat's navigation display.

## On deck

### goblin — small single-board computer

Small, numerous, green, and tinkerers. Tolkien wrote that "wheels and engines and
explosions always delighted them" and that they likely invented many of the
machines that have since troubled the world, which is the right spirit for a
board you solder things to.

### wormtongue — work laptop

Gríma Wormtongue was chief counsellor to King Théoden of Rohan. He had the
king's ear, sat at his table, and shaped every decision in Edoras, all while
quietly taking his orders from Saruman. That is a consultant's work laptop:
it sits in the client's meetings and advises their leadership, but its real
management chain runs back to the firm.
